# 1. SER fundamentals

Speech emotion recognition (SER) uses cues in speech to estimate expressed emotion. Automatic speech recognition (ASR) identifies the sequence of sounds and words spoken. The same recording can be input to either task, with different outputs. [S01](../sources/S01.md), §1, pp. 1–2 and §4, p. 8.

The review describes changes in fundamental frequency (F0), signal intensity, and timing as cues that can vary with emotion in complex ways. It discusses higher average F0 in high-arousal speech and gives both anger and happiness as examples. A higher pitch alone therefore gives no unique emotion label. [S01](../sources/S01.md), §§2.2–2.3, pp. 3–4.

**Illustrative example.** Imagine the phrase “I got it” spoken twice. An ASR system could produce the same words both times, while a SER system could assign different labels.
